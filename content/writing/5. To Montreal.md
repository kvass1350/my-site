I look out the window and see the pale, bright full moon. I find myself drawn to it, unreasonably so. Day gave way to night a while ago, and the air is getting colder. I'm on a double-decker bus, and in the quiet I can hear the rumble of the tires, the vibration of the road beneath us, the muffled chatter from the row in front, and the occasional whoosh of passing cars. It isn't the most spacious bus, but the cramped quarters give this trip a certain flavor.

It's quite dark outside. Only the silhouettes of trees remain, and even those slip out of focus before I can hold them.  They fly by in the foreground and slip into the past, into all that's been left behind.

When I try to look closer, the corner of my eye catches my own reflection, or rather, the absence of one; in the darkness, there is no reflection of myself to see.

So my eyes trace back to the moon. Sheer clouds drift across and veil it, and then it emerges, clear and bright again. The bus passes several gas stations, then streetlights, and billboards too. Sometimes it passes expansive fields that seem to stretch infinitely, with a few red and white lights lining the horizon, blinking.

I can't tell where I am anymore. The scenery has blurred into sameness. All I know is that I've been on this bus for far too long, and my butt hurts.

Road trips like this somehow always make me a little melancholic.
