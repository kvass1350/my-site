Whenever I have the time, I like to spend my days at libraries. For the past two days, I have practically resided at the [^1]Shanghai Library. 

I like to gaze out of the large, wide windows at the very end of its floors, where you have to look beyond rows upon rows of people sitting at their desks with their heads down, before finally arriving at this three-story-high glass window. It feels like a pleasant little treat for the eyes after extended hours of typing away in front of a computer screen. Outside the window stretches a vast blue sky, with white clouds scattered across. In the foreground are trees — green trees — occupying the bottom edge of the frame; beyond them are a few skyscrapers and residential buildings jutting out here and there.

Despite how populated the floors are and the constant flow of people coming and going, the space remains surprisingly silent. Only footsteps, coughs, and the occasional sound of pages turning surface briefly before dissolving back into the air. Even the children here are quietly engrossed in their books or homework. In fact, you can almost always spot a child tucked away in a corner between the shelves, holding a book or two, as you meander through the space. 

I still find it quite surreal that I am once again here in Shanghai. Oddly, as my days here go by, I find a slightly unsettling feeling gradually arising within me. I suppose it comes from the conflicting sentiments occupying my subconscious: returning here reminds me too much of my past, yet because I have been away for too long, I no longer feel a sense of belonging to that past anymore. 

My reminiscences of this place seem to have passed their best-before dates. Perhaps my impressions of this city before I left were simply incomplete or inaccurate, and perhaps the city itself has not changed as much as I thought. Regardless, over these past years, something about this place no longer matches the image I once held of it. Things must have changed. Everything feels more commercialized, more populated, and bigger, with almost too much going on all at once. The certain sense of autheticity and simplicity that I used to remember -- the local longtang culture -- now seems to have receded beneath all that noise. 

It is a strange feeling when the city where you spent your entire childhood gradually becomes a place that feels both familiar and unfamiliar at the same time. I still haven't lost my Shanghainese, but I don't know if that still means anything anymore. Do I still "belong" to Shanghai? Does Shanghai still "belong" to me?
