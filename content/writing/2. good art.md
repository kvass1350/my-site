
It is almost impossible to critique art, or to distinguish "good" art from "bad" art, as all concievable criterias have been demolished and nullified ever since the moment Duchamp smuggled the urinal into the exhibition. As we move forward into the contemporary art scene, the emphasis on conteptual meaning behind artworks has seen a rise in popularity and authority. but sometimes i wonder if by those standards, we are conflating the artist's ability to write, to concieve of narratives and craft metaphors and symbolisms, with the artist's artistic competence in creating art. 

 It has become impossible to objectivly judge art peices like how scientists judge experimental results. With the loss of objectivity in the critical scene, the voice of critique and value setting in the world of fine arts no longer belongs to the common person, but to distant authorities, institutions and captialistic forces.

### II. Wake up! 
#### i. Force
But what differentiates greatness from good? Above all themes listed as per the last section, I think the core principle to what differentiates great art from good art is the force a piece is capable of exerting -- namely it's capability to "awaken" the viewer from their current state of being. Great art can break the "fourth wall", and leave a trancending impression upon the viewer that drives them to a state of revelation and awe, or even further, transformation and action. This ability to forge a connection, to awaken something within people - be it memory, passion, will to live, help people see what is forgotten/ missed/ overlooked- or its ablity to poke fun at reality is extremely powerful. 

We are constantly sleep walking - very few of us are "awake"; I've been asleep for a majority of my life. However, when I encounter great works and immerse myself in the world they construct, I sense a force stemming from the work urging to wake me up. Take a popular example, the maxtrix. trying to make me be more vigilant, critical of reality, to not easily accept this manufactured reality. few more examples that gave me that feeling:

### so what
#### i. Defense
I think this is precisely why the arts is so important. Music, film, literature -- At least to me, They're all a line of defense to my soul, to keep me sane, and to some degree, protect my mind, and remind me of my exsistentce as a person, not as a replaceable part to the societal machines. To some sense art keeps me grounded. At least this is partially how I view art, and how art has shaped my perspective of the world, and my life. 

I've seen lot of people expressing that they feel unfree / constrained by society. I suppose in that sense, much of us aren't truly living. For me, I've constantly felt like being in the cycle of short increments of soberness followed by numbness - I am probably asleep most of the time. I cherish the times when I am sober, and i try my best to make them remain as long as possible. it's very hard to stay awake. There are so many forces trying to put us back to sleep. we need to constantly be awakened, again and again, try to stay awake as long as possible while living this life.
