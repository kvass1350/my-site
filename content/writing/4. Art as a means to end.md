By “instrumentalizing” art, I mean the process of making art answerable to external measures of usefulness, productivity, profitability, and achievement.

Making art a "quantifiable", "measurable", or "achievement-based" practice is evil and unjust. 

They defy the purpose of art, extricating the artist's voice and presence from thier works, leaving behind an empty shell of blandness. But, unfortunately, these are the rules to the game of reality. Despite how much we wish for complete freedom from those standards, we are not in a utopia insulated from reality. If one wishes to achieve social success to any extent, the "quantification" of achievement becomes impossible to evade. Afterall, the measurability of the impact, the percieved value of you and to your work is the language spoken by society, and now you must speak it too. 

Competions, residency applications, grants, exhibitions, follower count -- expand your audience, make big sales, prove your worth! but i suppose maybe sometimes you have to play the game for the sake of winning it, such that you are given permission to buy yourself the leeway to ignore them. that bothers me.  

trying to explain to "non-art people" what your art can do for them, and how your art could lead to an increase of profit or be useful to their organization can be quite miserable and soul wracking. 

To "instrumentalize" art is to ma art answerable to external measures of usefulness, productivity, profitability, and achievement. It is not just about monetizing. But anything involved with creating for the sake of others rather than yourself, and in the process having to "sell" yourself. I think of this as “job-ifying” art—not simply monetizing it, but subjecting an inherently personal practice to the expectations and incentives of a job.

To get a job, one has to "sell/pitch" themselves to others, convincing the the other party how "useful" they are, and how big of an impact they can bring. In the context of getting a job at a law firm, becoming an SWE, or an investment banker, these things make sense. But, in the context of art, this objective becomes a detriment. Producing art is an act of self reflection, not an act of productivity in the traditional sence. 

Art inherently just does not align with the goals of the modern social machines. They don't exactly directly contribute to making the world go round. They're not necessariy the infrasturcture. they're a bit more of a "luxury".

I prefer creating art under the circumstances of not having the pressure to monetize, or to please anyone in any sense, and I imagine many artists feel the same way. For instance, Taking commissions is not exactly my favourite activity, nor is creating art for the sake of working for others. i prefer to create art in the most selfish sense - that is to express myself unapologetically. 

material and social success can come as a by-product, but they simply cannot be the intent. Yet, by jobifying art, by being in the art indurstry, the artist has no choice, but to let those goals lead their practice (maybe to different extents, but those pressures are certainly always present). but what else can one do? artists have to feed themselves too; they must pay the bills, and if they wish to rely on their art as a source of income, it is inexorable that they must start caring about those "numbers" consciously or unconsioucly...

I hope to pursue Art as an end in itself
