
Across the street, and beneath the sign, was a convenience store tucked between a row of small shops. As the little [^1][Ampelmännchen](https://www.ampelmann.de/en/) flashed green, we crossed. Peering into the store, my eyes naturally drifted to the store owner. He glanced up briefly before returning his attention to his phone. Hidden behind cluttered shelves, he sat in the corner smoking while playing some video on his phone at medium volume. He wore a threadbare T-shirt and had an unkept beard. 

We stepped inside. To the right was a wall of refrigerators stocked with a wide variety of drinks. We reached into the shelves and picked out a [^2]German beer that Google had recommended.

Back outside, I took a sip. The liquid ran down my throat. It was cold, wheaty, and sharply carbonated. After finishing the beer, we left the empty bottle in a crate by the street.

 It was my first time trying (German) beer!

[^1]: The little traffic-light man wearing a hat shown on pedestrian signals in Berlin and across Germany. The figure was designed by East German traffic psychologist Karl Peglau in Berlin, which was then divided into East & West Berlin. Apparently, after the Berlin Wall fell and the German reunification in 1990, many East German symbols were gradually replaced with the standardized West German traffic-light figure. The Ampelmännchen was initially headed for extinction. But people *really* liked it. There was a massive public campaign to preserve it, and the figure gradually became associated not just with East Germany, but with Berlin itself and with Otalgic -- the nostalgia for certain aspects of former East German culture. Eventually, authorities decided to keep it. 

[^2]: We got a Lagerbier Hell, and a Giesinger Bräu. I remember liking Lagerbier Hell better.
