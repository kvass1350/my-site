
Across the street, and beneath the sign, a convenience store sat wedged between a row of small shops. As the little [^1][Ampelmännchen](https://www.ampelmann.de/en/) flashed green, we crossed. 

Peering into the store, my eyes naturally drifted to the store owner - some instict, maybe. He sat half-swallowed by shelves, smoking, a video playing low and tinny from his phone. He glanced up at us for a moment, then let his attention sink back into the screen. 

We stepped inside. To the right was a wall of refrigerators stocked with a wide variety of drinks. We reached into the shelves and picked out a [^2]German beer that the Google algorithms had recommended.

Back outside, I took a sip. The liquid was cold, wheaty, and sharply carbonated. We finished it walking down the midnight streets of Belin, under the the beer, we left the empty bottle in a crate by the street.

 It was my first time trying (German) beer!

[^1]: The little traffic-light man wearing a hat shown on pedestrian signals in Berlin and across Germany. The figure was designed by East German traffic psychologist Karl Peglau in Berlin, which was then divided into East & West Berlin. After the Berlin Wall fell and the German reunification in 1990, many East German symbols were gradually replaced with the standardized West German traffic-light figure. The Ampelmännchen was initially headed for extinction. But people *really* liked it. There was a massive public campaign to preserve it, and the figure gradually became associated not just with East Germany, but with Berlin itself and with Otalgic -- the nostalgia for certain aspects of former East German culture. Eventually, authorities decided to keep it. 

[^2]: We got a Lagerbier Hell, and a Giesinger Bräu. I remember liking Lagerbier Hell better.
