The balcony windows had been open since last night, letting in the howling wind and relentless rain. I woke up at 11 a.m. to find that the nocturne-like rain from midnight had already swelled into full-fledged symphony of clamor. 

As the rain abated around noon, I dragged myself out of bed and walked to the coffee shop downstairs. After settling my backpack and belonings on the round, wooden table in one of the corners of the shop, I sank into my seat with a warm coffee in hand, watching the trees sway and beads run down the window panes. I wonder if there is a word for this - the feeling that pertains to the warmth and the certain unnamable feeling of being indoors during extreme weather. There must be.

As I gazed away out the windows, a few memories throughout this trip surfaced. They were warm moments, times when I'd been taken care of. I've been quite lucky to have had this feeling bestowed upon me not once, but a few times recently. 

Typhoon days like these come unannounced; they can startle you quite a bit. I am thankful to the people who have offered me shelter through them.
